insight: ChatGPT ads that get clicked are clear, specific and useful, not catchy. OpenAI's own guidance favours benefit-focused copy that explains what you offer, who it's for and when it helps. Write several distinct variations per offer, describe real customer needs in your context hints, and send people to the most relevant page, not your homepage.

## The problem

Most advertisers bring their Google or Meta habits to ChatGPT: short keyword-stuffed headlines, urgency, "click here." But people seeing a ChatGPT ad are in the middle of asking for help. An ad that reads like a pushy banner feels out of place, and OpenAI's platform rewards relevance, not volume.

This guide covers what OpenAI recommends for titles, copy, context hints, images and landing pages, with examples from a made-up brand.

## The short answer

Write ads that answer "what is this, who is it for, and why would it help right now?" Keep the title short and informative, use the copy to add a new detail, and create several different angles for each offer. Then support those ads with specific context hints and a landing page that matches the promise.

## How is writing ChatGPT ads different from Google or Meta ads?

[OpenAI's ad creation guide](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001212-create-ads-for-chatgpt-ads) says ChatGPT ads need a different approach from keyword-based platforms. Instead of matching ads to a short search query, ChatGPT looks at the intent of the conversation and other relevance signals.

In practice, that means:

- **No keyword stuffing.** There are no keywords to match. The platform reads meaning.
- **Usefulness over urgency.** OpenAI says ads should feel helpful to what the user is trying to do.
- **Relevance affects cost.** ChatGPT uses a relevance-weighted auction, so a better-matched ad can compete without simply bidding more. Our [pricing and bidding guide](https://uptrixtechnologies.com/blog/chatgpt-ads-pricing) explains how.

## How do you write a strong ad title?

OpenAI's advice for titles is to spell out the value clearly, keep them concise, and choose informative details over broad marketing language. The recommended length is **16 to 24 characters** (50 maximum).

Examples for our made-up brand, Example Academy, an online English course:

| Weak title | Why it's weak | Stronger title |
|---|---|---|
| Best Courses Online!! | Vague, hype, no clear offer | Spoken English Classes (22) |
| Online English Course | Clear, but says nothing specific | Interview English Prep (22) |

Numbers in brackets are character counts. Avoid claims you can't back up, like "best" or "number one." OpenAI's ad policies prohibit misleading or unfounded claims.

## How do you write ad copy that adds value?

OpenAI says ad copy should **complement the title**, not repeat it, and should expand on benefits, features or use cases in plain language. The recommended length is **32 to 48 characters** (100 maximum).

| Weak copy | Stronger copy |
|---|---|
| Click here now for amazing deals on courses! (44) | Practise interview English with live tutors. (44) |
| Improve your English with our English course. | Evening classes for working adults. (35) |

The stronger versions tell the reader who it's for and what they'll actually get. Each could pair with a different title to create another variation.

## How many ad variations should you write?

More than one. OpenAI's guidance is to **build for coverage**: a larger, more varied set of ads for each offer helps its platform find more conversations where your ad is relevant.

The key word is *distinct*. OpenAI says each title and copy variation should bring a **different angle**, not reword the same message. For Example Academy, angles could be:

- **The goal:** preparing for job interviews
- **The format:** live evening classes
- **The audience:** working adults
- **The flexibility:** weekend batches

Inside an ad group, OpenAI also recommends including multiple ads to test different approaches to the same theme.

## How do you write context hints that match real conversations?

Context hints sit at the ad group level and describe when your ads may be useful. [OpenAI's ad group guide](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001211-create-ad-groups-for-chatgpt-ads) recommends clear, specific phrases about what you offer, who it helps, or when it's useful.

![Vague versus specific context hints: english course versus working adults preparing for job interviews who want to practise spoken English in live evening classes, and OpenAI's example of running shoes versus cushioned everyday running shoes for beginners training for their first 5K](/blogs/chatgpt-ads/chatgpt-ads-context-hints-examples.webp)

OpenAI's own example: "cushioned everyday running shoes for beginners training for their first 5K" is more useful than "running shoes."

Three rules to follow:

- **Describe a need, not a keyword.** Hints are not exact-match keywords and don't guarantee delivery.
- **Add what the ad doesn't say.** OpenAI suggests using hints for context beyond your title and copy.
- **Split different needs into different ad groups.** OpenAI's example is a plumber with separate groups for emergency repairs and preventive maintenance.

## What makes a good ChatGPT ad image?

OpenAI recommends simple, relevant images that support the ad's message, and advises against abstract or cluttered visuals. Where possible, the image should match the title and copy.

Technical specs, from OpenAI's launch guide: **PNG or JPG, square, no larger than 1200 x 1200.**

Two practical tips:

- Use a clear product photo or a simple scene showing the benefit.
- Don't design an image that looks like part of ChatGPT itself. OpenAI's ad policies ban ads that imitate its interface.

## Why does the landing page matter so much?

The landing page is part of the ad. OpenAI's guidance is to:

- **Link to the most relevant page**, such as a product or collection page, rather than your homepage.
- **Keep the ad and page closely connected**, so people move smoothly from interest to action.
- **Add UTM tags** to each ad's link for tracking.
- **Keep pages reachable**, and don't block OpenAI's crawlers (OAI-AdsBot and OAI-SearchBot).

Consistency matters for approval too. OpenAI's ad policies say the ad and landing page must match, so an approved ad can't lead to a page with content that isn't allowed.

## What gets ChatGPT ads rejected?

Based on [OpenAI's ad policies](https://openai.com/policies/ad-policies/), common reasons include:

- **Misleading claims** about pricing, results, affiliations or comparisons
- **Offensive or shocking language**, even in product names
- **Imitating ChatGPT's interface**
- **Restricted categories**, such as finance, health and legal services outside the US
- **Landing pages that don't match the ad** or can't be reviewed

If your category is borderline, check the rules in our [main ChatGPT ads guide](https://uptrixtechnologies.com/blog/chatgpt-ads) before writing anything.

## Is there a shortcut for writing ads?

Two, but both need your review.

- **Suggested ad drafts:** OpenAI says Ads Manager may offer "Add new ad," which prefills a draft from your website's existing metadata. It doesn't write new copy with AI.
- **AI creative help:** OpenAI announced in September 2026 that Ads Manager can suggest copy and images based on your landing page, which you edit before using.

Either way, apply the rules above before you publish.
